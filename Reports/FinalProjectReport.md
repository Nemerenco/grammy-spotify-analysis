### Summary:
This project aims to explore the relationship between artists who are GRAMMY award winners and their listeners' trends on Spotify, specifically their follower counts. The primary research question for this project is: "How does winning a GRAMMY award influence an artist's popularity on Spotify, as measured by the number of followers?" In order to answer this question, I combined data from the GRAMMY dataset from Kaggle and the Spotify API. The main motivation for this project is to see whether there is a clear correlation between winning a GRAMMY award and the number of Spotify followers an artist has. The analysis revealed that while there is a positive correlation between winning a GRAMMY and having a high number of Spotify followers, it is not a definitive indicator of popularity. There are artists who have won multiple GRAMMYs but have a relatively low number of Spotify followers, and vice versa. Having a high number of followers on Spotify does not always correlate with winning a GRAMMY. The project involved data collection, cleaning, analysis, visualization, and automation to provide insights on the relationship between GRAMMY winners and their Spotify listeners. ### Findings:
The GRAMMY dataset is clean and well-structured, with no missing values or inconsistencies so I did not have to do any preprocessing on it. The Spotify dataset also didn't require much preprocessing because I called the API to get the information I needed directly. I used the Spotify API to get the number of followers for each artist, their genre, and other relevant information. I then combined the two datasets based on the artist's name. After combining the datasets, I conducted exploratory data analysis to identify patterns and trends. I used Python libraries such as Pandas, and Matplotlib, to analyze the data and create visualizations. The analysis revealed that while there is a positive correlation between winning a GRAMMY and having a high number of Spotify followers, it is not a definitive indicator of popularity. There are artists who have won multiple GRAMMYs but have a relatively low number of Spotify followers, and vice versa. Having a high number of followers on Spotify does not always correlate with winning a GRAMMY. For example, Taylor Swift, Ed Sheeran and Billie Eilish are the three artists who have the highest numbers of Spotify followers but have won fewer GRAMMYs compared to other artists as they don't even make it in the top 30 artists with the most GRAMMY wins. This suggests that while winning a GRAMMY can boost an artist's visibility and popularity, it is not the only factor that determines their success on Spotify. Similarly, Aretha Franklin, who has the most GRAMMY wins, has a relatively low number of Spotify followers (under 15 million), compared to other artists. This indicates that the relationship between GRAMMY wins and Spotify followers is complex and influenced by various factors and not solely dependent on the number of awards won. I wrote a script to automate the data analysis and visualization process using Snakemake. The script includes steps for data cleaning, preprocessing, analysis, and visualization. It produces two dot plot visualizations showing the relationship between the number of GRAMMY wins and Spotify followers for artists. The first plot shows the top 30 artists with the most GRAMMY wins and their corresponding Spotify followers, while the second plot shows the top 30 artists with the most Spotify followers and their corresponding GRAMMY wins. The visualizations help illustrate the finding that followers on Spotify do not always correlate with winning a GRAMMY as each plot shows different artists in the top 30 lists.
